Where the money goes

Synthea healthcare claims, 2020–2024. $99.1 billion billed across 68.6 million claims; $72.7 billion of it attributable to a clinical condition.

1. Twenty conditions account for 91% of it

Total billed per condition. The top 20 cover 90.8% of condition-attributable spend and 66.6% of all spend.

Normal pregnancy alone is 39.8% — more than the next three combined.

2. Two different kinds of expensive

Patients treated against cost per patient. Both axes are logarithmic; bubble size is total billed. Top-left means few patients at enormous cost each; bottom-right means many patients at modest cost.

broad population severity-filtered (≥90% inpatient) bubble size = total billed
Gingivitis and lung carcinoma bill nearly the same in total, from opposite corners: 800,475 patients at $10,661 versus 2,384 at $1,416,145.

Orange points are recorded almost exclusively on inpatient stays, so their patient counts are the hospitalised subset rather than everyone with the condition — their cost per patient is not comparable to the blue points.

3. Do hospitals charge differently for the same condition?

Cost per claim across hospitals, one row per condition, log scale. The thin line spans min to max, the bar is the middle 50% of hospitals, the dot is the median. Ratio at right is max ÷ min. Hospitals need ≥30 claims for the condition to be included.

middle 50% of hospitals median hospital thin line = full range
For most conditions the middle 50% is narrow — typical hospitals bill nearly identically. The huge ratios come from a handful of outlier sites, not from widespread price differences.

Payer type was tested and made almost no difference — median spread across Government, Commercial and Self-Pay is 1.06×. This dataset has no negotiated rates (adjustments are zero on all 887M rows), so the same service is billed identically whoever pays; payers differ in what share they cover, not what they are charged.
