Only dental is both a big bill and a big share

Synthea healthcare claims, 2020–2024. Each circle is one of the 15 types of care. Position left to right is what patients paid in total; position up the page is how much of the bill they carried; circle area is how many people were affected.

Everything else is either a lot of money at a small share, or a big share of very little

Circle size is the number of people affected, from 13,122 for infections to 938,009 for dental. The horizontal axis is a log scale — patient spending runs from $16M to $4.9B, a 300-fold range a linear axis would flatten into one cluster.

The top-right corner is the one worth acting on, and only dental is in it. $3.30B paid by patients at 27.9% of the bill across 938,009 people. Maternity is larger in dollars but patients carry only 17.0% of it; diabetes and metabolic care has a higher share at 36.6% but patients pay just $159M. Dental is the only type of care that is simultaneously expensive to patients, expensive as a proportion, and widespread.

Limitations

  • Log x-axis — equal spacing means ten times the money, not ten times more.
  • Circle area, not radius, is proportional to people — sizes are comparable, not readable as values.
  • Shares are blends across insurance type; commercial patients pay far more than government ones.
  • Dollar amounts inflated by flat procedure pricing; positions and rankings hold.
